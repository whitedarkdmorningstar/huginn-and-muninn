# Shock

> Shock is a clinical condition characterized by failue to adequately perfuse and oxygenate vital organs.

## Clinical Signs

> ↑ HR, ↑ RR, ↓ BP, ↓ UO, altered mental status

1. **Hypotension**: Systolic blood pressure \< 90 mmHg or mean arterial pressure \< 65 mmHg
1. **Tachycardia**: Heart rate \> 100 bpm
1. **Altered consciousness and/or fainting**:
   - Poor peripheral pefusion
   - Cool peripheies
   - Clammy/sweaty skin
   - Pallor
   - Reduced capillary return (CRT \> 2 seconds)
   - Warm peripheries in the early phase of distributive shock (e.g. sepsis)
1. **Oliguria**: Urine output \< 0.5 mL/kg/hr (\< 50 mL/hr in adults)
1. **Tachypnea**: Respiratory rate \> 20 breaths/min

## Classification

1. Pump failure
   1. **Cardiogenic Shock**
      - Due to pump failure of the heart.
      - Causes: `Myocardial infarction`, `myocarditis`, `arrhythmias`, `valvular disease`.
   1. **Obstructive Shock**
      - Due to obstruction in the circulation.
      - Causes: `Pulmonary embolism`, `cardiac tamponade`, `tension pneumothorax`.
1. Peripheral circulatory failure
   1. **Hypovolemic Shock**
      - Due to decreased intravascular volume.
      - Causes: `Haemorrhage` (trauma, GI bleeding), `dehydration` (vomiting, diarrhea, burns).
   1. **Distributive Shock**
      - Due to vasodilation and maldistribution of blood.
      - Causes:
      - `Septic Shock`: Infection leading to systemic inflammatory response.
      - `Anaphylactic Shock`: Severe allergic reaction.
      - `Neurogenic Shock`: Spinal cord injury disrupting autonomic control.
   1. **Endocrine Shock**
      - Due to severe endocrine dysfunction leading to circulatory collapse.
      - Examples:
      - `Adrenal Crisis` (Acute Adrenal Insufficiency) → Lack of cortisol → Hypotension, hypoglycemia, electrolyte imbalance.
      - `Myxedema Coma` (Severe hypothyroidism) → Hypothermia, bradycardia, hypotension.
      - `Thyroid Storm` (Severe hyperthyroidism) → Tachycardia, high-output heart failure.
   1. **Iatrogenic**
      - `Drugs` (e.g. `antihypertensives`, `diuretics`, `sedatives`)

## Management

### General Management

1. Assessemnt and resuscitation simultaneouly
1. **ABC** (Airway, Breathing, Circulation) are priorities
1. High flow **oxygen** by mask
1. Adequate **IV access** and take blood samples (`FBC`, `Glucose`)
1. Monitor **vital signs** (BP, PR, RR, SpO2, temperature)
1. Monitor **ECG** and chest X-ray
1. **Urinary catheter** for urine output monitoring
1. Control any external bleeding by applying pressure to the wound
1. `IV crystalloids 0.9% saline infusion (20 mL/kg)` as a bolus for shock associated with hypovolemia or distributive shock. Repeat bolus if necessary.
1. Look for and treat specifically the causes of shock

#### Anaphylactic Shock

1. Stop further administration of suspected agents (e.g. drugs, contrast media, blood products)
1. Give 100% oxygen
1. Open and maintain airway:
   - If upper airway oedema is present, consider emergency intubation or surgical airway (e.g. cricothyroidotomy)
1. Give `IM adrenaline 0.5 mg` (1:1000) into the anterolateral aspect of the thigh, repeat after 5 min if no improvement
1. In profound shock or immediately life-threatening situations such as cardiac arrest, follow cardiac arrest guidelines
1. `IV fluids` (e.g. `0.9% saline`) 1-2 L to maintain blood pressure if hypotension persists after adrenaline
1. `H1 blockers` (e.g. `chlorphenamine 10-20 mg slow IV`)
1. `H2 blockers` (e.g. `ranitidine 50 mg IV`)
1. `IV corticosteroids` (e.g. `hydrocortisone 200 mg IV`) to prevent biphasic or protracted reactions
1. Admit/observe for at least 4-6 hours after all symptoms have settled

### Septic Shock

#### Classification

1. Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome (SIRS)
   - 2 or more of the following:
     1. Temperature \> 38°C or \< 36°C
     1. Heart rate \> 90 bpm
     1. Respiratory rate \> 20 breaths/min or PaCO₂ \< 32 mmHg
     1. WBC count \> 12,000/mm³ or \< 4,000/mm³ or \> 10% bands
1. Sepsis
   - SIRS + A confirmed or suspected infection
1. Severe Sepsis
   - Sepsis + Organ dysfunction, hypoperfusion, or hypotension (SBP \< 90 mmHg) or Lactate \> 4 mmol/L
1. Septic Shock
   - Sepsis + Persistent hypotension despite adequate fluid resuscitation

#### Management [#septic]

1. Initial fluid resuscitation of `20 ml/kg of 0.9% normal saline`
1. Look for the obvious source of infection
1. Give `IV antibiotics`:
   - `IV ceftriaxone` for meingococcal sepsis
   - Consider combination therapy if no obvious source (e.g. `Amoxicillin/clavulanic acid` + `Gentamycin` + `Metronidazole`)
1. `Inotrope` support:
   - `IV infusion Noradrenaline 0.5-20 mcg/min`
1. Early referal to the hospital with the higher-level hospital

### Cardiogenic Shock

#### Clinical Features

1. Hypotension (SBP \< 90 mmHg)
1. Tachycardia (HR \> 100 bpm)
1. Cool, clammy skin
1. Oliguria (UO \< 0.5 mL/kg/hr)
1. Pulmonary congestion (dyspnea, orthopnea, rales)
1. Altered mental status (confusion, agitation, lethargy)
1. Signs of low cardiac output (weak peripheral pulses, narrow pulse pressure)
1. Left ventricular dysfunction (e.g. S3 gallop, displaced apical impulse)
1. Jugular venous distension (JVD) and peripheral edema (in right-sided heart failure)
1. ECG changes: Arrhythmias, ST-segment changes, Q waves
1. Chest X-ray: Pulmonary edema, cardiomegaly, pleural effusions

#### Management [#cardiogenic]

1. IV access for mild hypotension without pulmonary congestion, a small fluid challenge 250-500 ml may be considered
1. Consider `inotrope` support:
   - `IV infusion Dobutamine 2.5-20 mcg/kg/min`
   - `IV infusion Dopamine 2.5-20 mcg/kg/min`
   - `IV infusion Noradrenaline 0.5-20 mcg/min` if hypotension persists
1. Do not give `beta-blockers` or `calcium channel blockers` in acute cardiogenic shock

### Hypovolemic Shock

#### Causes of Intravascular Volume Loss

1. **Haemorrhage**:
   1. Thorax
      - Lung injury
      - Aortic injury
      - Massive haemoptysis
   1. Abdomen/Pelvis/Retroperitoneum
      - Solid organ injury (e.g. liver, spleen)
      - Vascular injury (e.g. trauma, ruptured aneurysm)
      - GI haemorrhage (e.g. peptic ulcer, varices)
      - Gynaecological (e.g. ectopic pregnancy, postpartum haemorrhage)
   1. Orthopaedic
      - Pelvic fracture
      - Long bone fracture
   1. Extrimity and skin surface
      - Lacerations
      - Amputations
1. **Non-haemorrhagic**:
   - Gastrointestinal losses (vomiting, diarrhea)
   - Renal losses (diuretics, osmotic diuresis)
   - Skin losses (burns, excessive sweating)
   - Third-space losses (ascites, peritonitis)

#### Clinical Features [#hypovolemic]

#### Management [#hypovolemic]

1. Airway: Intubate and ventilate if necessary
1. Breathing:
   - Give high-flow oxygen by mask 10-15 L/min
   - Quickly treat immediately life-threatening injuries (e.g. tension pneumothorax, flail chest, massive haemothorax, foreign body in the airway)
1. Vascular access
   - 2 or more IV access with large bore cannula
   - Blood G&M, RBS, Pulse oximetry, BP, CRT, ECG moinitoring
   - Identify source of fluid loss
1. Fluid resuscitation
   - `Isotonic crystaloid solution`: initial bolus 1-2 L NS → Reassess → Give second bolus 1-2 L
   - Control external blood loss by applying pressure packing or suturing
   - If haemodynamic status is not stablilized after the second bolus, the blood loss \> 15-20% of total blood volume **or** ongoing blood loss **or** both
1. Blood transfusion
   - `Trauma patient` still in `shock` with `minimal haemodynamic improvement` after `2-3 L of crystalloid infusion` needs a blood transfusion
   - When possilbe, type and cross-matched blood is preferable, otherwise type **O** Rh-negative blood
   - `Whole blood` or `Packed RBC`
1. Correct the cause of shock
