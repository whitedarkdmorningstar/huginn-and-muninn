# Cardiogenic Pulmonary Oedema

## Causes

- Left ventricular failure due to
  - Ischaemic heart disease
  - Cardiomyopathy
  - Myocarditis
  - Acute valve syndrome (e.g. IE or chordae/papillary muscle rupture)
  - Arrhythmia
  - Hypertensive emergency
  - Severe mitral stenosis

## Management

<Steps>

<Step>

### Position

- Sit the patient upright

</Step>

<Step>

### Oxygen

- If SaO2 \< 90%
- Too much oxygen in systemic circulation causes **vasoconstriction** but in pulmonary circulation causes **vasodilation**.

> Avoid supplement oxygen if not hypoxaemic as it causes vasoconstriction and may reduce cardiac output!

#### Continuous Positive Airway Pressue (CPAP)

- Consider in _patients who does not response to treatment_
- CPAP increase intrathoracic pressure → ↓ Venous return → ↓ Preload
- CPAP increase pressure gradiant between left ventricle and extrathoracic arteries → ↓ Afterload
- Consider **intubation** in _patients with persistent hypoxaemia on CPAP or persistent hypercapnia despite the administration of oxygen, morphine, diuretics and vasodilators_.

##### Contraindication for CPAP

- SBP \< 90 mmHg
- Facial trauma, Base of skull fracture
- Dementia resulting in intolerance of therapy
- Reduced conscious level
- Pneumothorax
- Type II respiratory failure, severe emphysema

##### Complications of CPAP

- Hypotension
- Aspiration
- Gastric distension
- Anxiety

##### When to Stop CPAP

- Continue until chest clear of rales or haemodynamically stable
- Wean airway pressure, wean supplemental oxygen, change to facemask
- ~~If no improvement after 30 min, CPAP should be stopped.~~

</Step>

<Step>

### IV Access

- Monitor ECG
- Treat any arrhythmias

</Step>

<Step>

### Diuresis

> `IV Furosemide 40-80 mg`

- For diuretic naive, IV bolus dose 0.5-2 mg/kg (`Furosemide 40-80 mg`)
- If taking regular oral diuretics, up to 2-3 times the previously ineffective oral dose may be needed
- In CKD, higher doses of diuretic are needed (2-5 times depending on CKD stage)

</Step>

<Step>

### Nitrate

- If SBP \> 90 mmHg, `GTN spray 2 pufss` **OR** `2 x 0.3 mg Tab Sublingual`
- `IV infusion GTN 50 mg in 50 mL of 0.9% saline 2-10 mg/hr` (titrate to SBP \> 90 mmHg)

</Step>

<Step>

### Cause

- History, examination and investigations
- Treat cause (e.g., ACS, hypertensive emergency)

</Step>

<Step>

### If no improvement

- Further dose of `IV Furosemide 40-80 mg`
- Increase `GTN infusion keeping SBP \> 90 mmHg`
- Consider CPAP (or BiPAP if type 2 respiratory failure) to improve ventilation by recruiting alveoli, driving fluid out of alvelar spaces and into vasculature
- Consider alternative diagnoses, e.g. Pulmonary embolism, Pneumonia, Aortic dissection

</Step>

<Step>

### If SBP \< 90 mmHg

- Treat as cardiogenic shock

</Step>

</Steps>
