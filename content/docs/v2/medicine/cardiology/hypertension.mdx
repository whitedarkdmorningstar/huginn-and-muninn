# Hypertension

## Definition

| Category                                                                        | Systolic BP                        | Diastolic BP                    |
| :------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | :--------------------------------- | :------------------------------ |
| **Blood pressure**<br/>Optimal<br/>Normal<br/>High normal                       | <br/>< 120<br/>< 130<br/>< 130-139 | <br/>< 80<br/>85<br/>< 85-89    |
| **Hypertension**<br/>Grade 1 (mild)<br/>Grade 2 (moderate)<br/>Grade 3 (severe) | <br/>140-159<br/>160-179<br/>≥ 180 | <br/>90-99<br/>100-109<br/>>110 |
| **Isolated systolic hypertension** <br/>Grade 1<br/>Grade 2                     | <br/>140-159<br/>≥ 160             | <br/>< 90<br/>< 90              |

<details>
<summary>Blood Pressure Formula</summary>

> BP = CO x SVR.

- BP = Blood Pressure
- CO = Cardiac Output
- SVR = Systemic Vascular Resistance

> CO = SV x HR.

- SV = Stroke Volume
- HR = Heart Rate

> SV = EDV - ESV.

- EDV = End-Diastolic Volume
- ESV = End-Systolic Volume

> MBP = DBP + 1/3(SBP - DBP).

- MBP = Mean Blood Pressure
- DBP = Diastolic Blood Pressure
- SBP = Systolic Blood Pressure

</details>

## Classification

1. Primary or Idiopathic or Essential Hypertension (95%)
1. Secondary Hypertension (5%)

### Causes of Secondary Hypertension

> A RED COP

1. **A**lcohol
1. **R**enal disease
   - Parenchymal renal disease:
     - Glomerular or tubulointerstitial disease
   - Polycystic kidney disease [^1]
   - Renal vascular disease:
     - Macrovascular (atherosclerosis, fbromuscular dysplasia or vasculitis)
     - Microvascular (small vessel vasculitis or thrombotic microangiopathies)
1. **E**ndocrine disease
   - Phaeochromocytoma [^4]
   - Primary hypothyroidism
   - Thyrotoxicosis
   - Hyperparathyroidism
   - Acromegaly
   - Conn syndrome (Primary hyperaldosteronism)
   - Glucocorticoid-suppressible hyperaldosteronism
   - Cushing syndrome
   - Liddle syndrome
1. **D**rugs
1. **C**oarctation of the aorta [^2]
1. **O**besity
1. **P**regnancy

## Complications

> HMOD (Hypertion Mediated Organ Damage or Hypertensive Target Organ Damage)

1. Brain
   - Stroke
   - Hypertensive encephalopathy
1. Eyes
   - Hypertensive Retinopathy (silver wing, arteriovenous nipping, haemorrhage & exudates, papilloedema)
1. Heart
   - Left ventricular hypertrophy
   - Heart failure
   - Ventricular arrhythmias, Atrial fibrillation
   - Aortic dissection
   - Sudden cardiac death
1. Kidneys
   - Acute/Chronic kidney disease
1. Vessels
   - Transient ischemic attack (TIA)
   - Coronary artery disease (IHD, MI)
   - Peripheral arterial disease

<details>
<summary>Changes in vessels due to hypertension</summary>

1. Large arteries (> 1 mm in diameter):
   - Vessels dilate → Become tortuous
1. Small arteries (< 1 mm in diameter):
   - Arteriosclerosis → Lumen narrows → ~~Aneurysms~~
1. Plaques(Artheroma) in vessels → Narrow vessels → Worsen hypertension → More plaques → ↑Peripheral vascular resistance & ↓renal blood flow
1. ↓Renal blood flow → Activate RAAS (Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System) → **Hypertension**

</details>

<details>
<summary>RAA System</summary>

- Has 4 organs
- `Liver` produce **Angiotensinogen** protein
- ↓Renal blood flow or ↓blood volume & pressure → `Kidney` produces **Renin** → **Renin** converts **Angiotensinogen** to **Angiotensin I** → **ACE (Angiotensin-Converting-Enzyme)** converts **Angiotensin I** to **Angiotensin II** in `Lungs` → Action of **Angiotensin II** (_vasoconstriction, thirst_) → Release of **Aldosterone** from `Adrenal glands` → Action of **Aldosterone** (_salt and water reabsorption_) → ↑Blood volume and pressure
- RAAS can be activated in hypertension when renal blood flow is decreased

</details>

## Risk Factors

1. Non-modifiable risk factors
   - Genetics
   - Age
   - Sex
1. Modifiable risk factors
   - Smoking
   - Obesity
   - Hyperlipidaemia
   - High salt intake
   - Lack of exercise
   - High alcohol consumption
   - Stress
   - Diabetes Mellitus

## Clinical Features

1. **Asymptomatics**
1. **Features of underlying cause of hypertension**
   - Radio-femoral delay in coarctation of aorta
   - Enlarged kidneys in polycystic kidney disease
   - Abdominal bruits in renal artery stenosis
   - Characteristic facies and habitus of Cushing's syndrome
1. **Features of complications**
   - Signs of left ventricular hypertrophy, heart failure, MI
   - Impaired renal function
   - Stroke, aortic aneurysms
1. **Presence of comorbidities**
   - Diabetes
   - Hyperlipidaemia
   - Hyperuricemia
   - Ischemic heart disease
   - Cerebrovascular accident

## Investigations

<details>
<summary>How to measure blood pressure</summary>

- Use a **machine** that has been validated, well maintained and properly caliberated
- Measure **sitting** BP routinely, with additional **standing** BP in older and diabetic patients and those with possible postural hypotension; rest the patient for 2 minutes
- **Remove tight clothing** from the arm
- Support the **arm at the level of the heart**
- Use a **cuff of appropriate size** (the bladder must encompass more than two-thrids of the arm)
- **Lower** the pressure **slowly** (2 mmHg per second)
- **Read the BP** to the nearest 2 mmHg
- Use phase V (**disappearance of sounds**) to measure diastolic BP
- Take **two measurements** at each visit

</details>

### Investigation of Hypertension

- **Urinalysis** for blood, protein and glucose
- **Blood urea**, **electrolytes** and **creatinine** (Hypokalaemic alkalosis may indicate primary hyperaldosteronism but is usually due to diuretic therapy)
- **Serum total and HDL cholesterol** (High-density lipoprotein)
- **Thyroid function test**: To detect hypo- or Hyperthyroidism
- **12-lead ECG**: To detect myocardial ischaemia, left ventricular hypertrophy

### Specialized Investigation of Hypertension

> For _Patients \< 40 years of age_ or _Patients with resistant hypertension_.

- **Chest X-ray**: To detect cardiomegaly, heart failure, coarctation of the aorta
- **Echocardiogram**: To detect or quantify left ventricular hypertrophy
- **Ambulatory BP recording**: To assess borderline or 'white coat' hypertension
- **Renal ultrasound**: To detect possible renal disease
- **Renal angiography**: To detect or confirm renal artery stenosis
- **Urinary catecholamines**: To detect possible phaeochromocytoma
- **Urinary cortisol and dexamethasone suppression test**: To detect possible Cushing's syndrome
- **Plasma renin activity and aldosterone**: To detect possible primary aldosteronism
- **Urine HCG test**: To detect pregnancy
- **Blood glucose (Fasting, Random, OGTT), HbA1c**: To detect diabetes mellitus

## Management

### Optimal Target Blood Pressures

| Age            | Clinic BP | Ambulatory or home BP |
| :------------- | :-------- | :-------------------- |
| **< 80 years** | < 140/90  | < 135/85              |
| **≥ 80 years** | < 150/90  | < 140/85              |

### General Management

> Life-style interventions <br/>may obviate the need for drug therapy in patients with borderline hypertension,<br/> reduce the dose or the number of drugs required in patients with established hypertension, and <br />directly reduce cardiovascular risk.

> SAVED

1. `S`moking
   - Cessation of smoking
1. `A`lcohol
   - Reduction or cessation of alcohol consumption
1. `V`accination
   - Consideration of influenza and pneumococcal vaccination
1. `E`ducation
   - Explanation of nature of disease, treatment and self-help strategies
1. `E`xercise
   - Regular moderate exercise within limit
1. `D`iet
   - Good nutrition and weight reduction
   - Avoidance of salty foods

### Pharmacological Management

#### Thiazides Diuretics

- MOA: Unknown
- `Bendroflumethiazide` 2.5 mg daily, `Cyclopenthiazie` 0.5 mg
- Compelling indications: **Older patients**, **Isolated systolic hyertension**, **Heart failure**, **Secondary stroke prevention**
- Compelling contraindications: ~~Ghout~~

#### Loop Diuretics

- `Furosemide` 40 mg daily, `Bumetanide` 1 mg daily

#### ACE inhibitors

- `Enalapril` 5-40 mg daily, `Ramipril` 5-10 mg daily, `Lisinopril` 10-40 mg daily
- Compelling indications: **Heart failure**, **Left ventricular dysfunction**, **post-MI or established CAD**, **Type 1 diabetic nephropathy**, **Secondary stroke prevention**
- Possible indications: Chronic renal disease, Type 2 diabetic nephropathy
- Caution: _Renal impairment, Renal artery stenosis, Peripheral arterial disease_
- Compelling contraindications: ~~Pregnancy~~, ~~Renovascular disease~~
- Side Effects: Dry cough, Hypotension, Hyperkalaemia

#### Angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARB)

- `Valsartan` 40-160 mg daily, `irbesartan` 75-300 mg daily
- Compelling indications: **ACE inhibitors intolerance**, **Type 2 diabetic nephropathy**, **Hypertension with left ventricular hypertrophy**, **Heart failure in ACE-intolerant patients**, **after MI**
- Possible indications: Left ventricular dysfunction after MI, Intolerance of other anti-hypertensive drugs, Proteinuric or chronic renal disease, Heart failure
- Caution: _Renal impairment, Peripheral arterial disease_
- Compelling contraindications: ~~Pregnancy~~
- Side Effects: Hypotension, Hyperkalaemia

#### Calcium channel antagonists

1. Dihydropyridine
   - `Nifedipine` 30-90 mg daily, `Amlodipine` 5-10 mg daily
   - Compelling indications: **Older patients**, **Isolated systolic hypertension**
   - Possible indications: Angina
   - Side Effects: flushing, palpitations, fluid retention
1. Rate-limiting (Non-Dihydropyridine)
   - `Verapamil` 240-480 mg daily in divided dose, `Diltiazem` 200-500 mg daily
   - Compelling indications: **Angina**
   - Possible indications: Older patients
   - Caution: _Combination with beta-blockers_
   - Compelling contraindications: ~~AV block~~, ~~Heart failure~~
   - Side Effect: Bradycardia, Constipation (`verapamil`)

#### Beta-blockers

- No longer used as first-line anti-hypertensive therapy
- Selective (beta-1):\* (Preferred)
  - `Metoprolol` (100-200 mg daily), `Atenolol` (50-100 mg daily), `Bisoprolol` (5-10 mg daily)
- Non-selective:
  - (beta-1 & -2) `Propanolol`, `Sotalol`, `Timolol`
  - (alpha-1, beta-1 & -2) `Labetalol` (200 mg-2.4g daily in divided dose), `Carvedilol` (6.25-25 mg twice daily)
- Compelling indications: **MI**, **Angina**, **Stable heart failure**
- Caution: Stable heart failure, Peripheral arterial disease, Diabetes (except with CAD)
- Compelling contraindications: ~~Asthma or COPD~~, ~~Heart block~~
- Side Effects: vasodilation and bronchodilation (non-selectives)

#### Vasodilators

- Alpha-1-adrenoceptor antagonists:
  - `Prazosin` (0.5-20 mg daily in divided dose), `Indoramin` (25-100 mg twice daily), `Doxzosin` (1-16 mg daily)
- Direct vasodilators:
  - `Hydralazine` (25-100 mg twice daily), `Minoxidil` (10-50 mg daily)
- Side effects: first-dose, postural hypotension, headache, tachycardia, fluid retention, increase facial hair (`minoxidil`) unsuitable for feamle patient

#### Aspirin (Anti-platelet Therapy)

- Reduce cardiovascular risk
- Benefits for _hypertensive patients ≥ 50 years_ or _well-controlled BP and either target organ damage or diabetes or a 10-year CAD risk of at least 15%_
- Side effects: Bleeding, Intracerebral haemorrhage

#### Statins (Lipid-lowering Therapy)

- For hyperlipidaemia, _patients with established vascular disease_, _hypertension with a high risk of CVD_
- Reduce cardiovascular risk
- `atorvastatin`, `rosuvastatin`

<summary>Anti-hypertensive drug combinations</summary>

1. Step 1:
   - Aged < 55 yrs → `A`
   - Aged > 55 yrs → `C`
1. Step 2: `A` + `C`
1. Step 3: `A` + `C` + `D`
1. Step 4: **Resistant hypertension** (`A` + `C` + `D` + consider `further diuretic` or `α-` or `β-blocker`; Consider seeking export advice)

> `A` - ACE inhibitor or ARB <br/>`C` - Calcium channel antagonists <br/>`D` - Thiazide-type diuretic <br/>`further diuretic` - Low dose of spironolactone or high dose of thiazide-like diuretic.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Management Algorithm</summary>

**1. Clinic Blood Pressure < 140/90 mmHg (Normotensive)**

- Check blood pressure at least every 5 years, more often if blood pressure is close to 140/90 mmHg

**2. Clinic Blood Pressure ≥ 140/90 mmHg**

1. ABPM (or HBPM if ABPM is declined or not tolerated)
1. Assess cardiovascular risk and traget organ damage
   1. _If ABPM/HBPM < 135/85 mmHg_ (Normotensive):
      - ~~If evidence of traget organ damage~~: Consider alternative causes for target organ damage
      - Check blood pressure regularly
   1. _If ABPM/HBPM ≥ 135/85 mmHg_ (Stage 1 hypertension)
      - ~~If target organ damage present or 10-yr cardiovascular risk > 20%~~: **anti-hypertensive therapy**
      - ~~If < 40 yrs~~: Consider specialist referral
      - Life-style interventions
      - Patient education and interventions to support adherence to adherence to treatment
      - Annual review of care to monitor blood pressure, provide support and discuss lifestyle, symptoms and medication
   1. _If ABPM/HBPM ≥ 150/95 mmHg_ (Stage 2 hypertension)
      - **anti-hypertensive therapy**
      - Life-style interventions
      - Patient education
      - Annual review

**3. Clinic Blood Pressure ≥ 180/110 mmHg**

- _If accelerated hypertension [^3] or suspected phaeochromocytoma[^4]_: Refer same day for specialist care
- Otherwise, same management as **Clinic Blood Pressure ≥ 140/90 mmHg**

</details>

---

[^1]: **PKD (Polycystic kidney disease)**: Genetics (Autosomal dominant/recessive) disorder where inside kidneys is numerous fluid-filled cysts → Hypertension (earliest), Kidneys [Size↑, Pain, Failure, Stones, UTI]

[^2]: **Coarctation of aorta**: Congenital heart defect where aorta is narrow → hypertension (in older children & adults)

[^3]: **Accelerated Hyeprtension**: Signs of papilloedema or retinal haemorrhage

[^4]: **Phaeochromocytoma**: A rare tumor of adrenal gland that causes hypertension by secreting excess catecholamine hormones, such as epinephrine and norepinephrine
