# Functional Anatomy and Physiology

- Inspiration involves downward **contraction of the diaphragm** (innervated by the _phrenic nerves originating from C3, 4 and 5_) and upward, outward movement of the ribs, caused by **contraction of the external intercostal muscles** (innervated by _intercostal nerves originating from the thoracic spinal cord_)
- Expiration is largely passive, driven by elastic recol of the lungs.

- Maximum inspiratory volume is limited by the lung rather than the chest wall.
- The volume that can be exhaled is limited purely by the capacity of the expiratory muscles to distort the chest wall inwards.
- In ~~emphysema~~, loss of alveolar walls → unsupported small airways collapsed on expiration → air trapping → limit expiration at a high end-expiratory volume.
- Respiratory motor neurons in the posterior medulla oblongata are the origin of the respiratory cycle.

- Inhaled particulate matter is trapped in airway mucus and cleared by the mucociliary escalator.
- Impairment of mucociliary clearance predisposes towards ~~lower respiratory tract infections, including pneumonia~~, ~~cystic fibrosis~~ and ~~primary ciliary dyskinesia~~.

## Respiratory Function in Old Age

- ↓ in FEV<sub>1</sub>
  - Smoking accelerates the decline threefold on average.
  - Symptoms usually occur only when FEV<sub>1</sub> \< 50% of predicted.
- ↑ ventilation-perfusion mismatch
- ↓ responses to hypoxia and hypercapnia
- Impaired defences against infection
- ↓ in maximum oxygen uptake
