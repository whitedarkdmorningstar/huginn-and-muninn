# Investigation of Respiratory Disease

## Imaging

### 1. Chest X-ray

> PA CXR film provides information on the lung fields, heart, mediastinum, vascular structures and thoracic cage.

> Lateral CXR provides additional information, particularly if pathology is suspected behind the heart or deep in the diaphragmatic sulci.

![Normal chest X-ray](/v2/medicine/x-ray-normal-chest.png)

#### Common abnormalities on chest X-ray

##### Pulmonary and pleural shadowing

- **Consolidation**: infection, infarction, inflammation and, rarely bronchoalveolar cell carcinoma
- **Lobar collapse**: mucus plugging, tumour, compression by lymph nodes
- **Solitary nodule**
- **Multiple nodules**: miliary tuberculosis (TB), dust inhalation, metastatic malignancy, healed varicella pneumonia, rheumatoid disease
- Ring shadows, tramlines and tubular shadows: bronchiectasis
- **Cavitating lesions**: tumour, abscess, infarct, pneumonia (_Staphylococcus_/_Klebsiella_), granulomatosis with polyangiitis (GPA)
- Reticular, nodular and reticulonodular shadows: diffuse parenchymal lung
  disease, infection
- Pleural abnormalities: fluid, plaques, tumour

##### Increased translucency

- Bullae
- Pneumothorax
- Oligaemia

##### Hilar abnormalities

- **Unilateral hilar enlargement**: TB, lymphoma, lung cancer
- **Bilateral hilar enlargement**: TB, lymphoma, sarcoidosis, silicosis

##### Others

- Hiatus hernia
- Surgical emphysema

### 2. Computed tomography (CT)

- In cases of suspected lung cancer, CT is central to both diagnosis and staging and facilitates percutaneous needle biopsy

### 3. Positron emission tomography (PET)

- It is useful in the staging of mediastinal lymph nodes and distal metastatic disease in patients with lung cancer and in the investigation of pulmonary nodules

### 4. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)

- In distinguishing benign from malignant pleural disease and in delineating invasion of the chest wall or diaphragm by tumour

### 5. Ultrasound

- To safely guide pleural aspiration, biopsy and intercostal chest drain
  insertion
- To guide needle biopsy of superficial lymph node or chest wall masses

## Endoscopic Examination

### 1. Laryngoscopy

- In cases of suspected intermittent laryngeal ostruction (ILO), when paradoxical movement of the vocal cords may mimic asthma
- Left-sided lung tumours may involve the left recurrent laryngeal nerve, paralysing the left vocal cord, hoarse voice and a 'bovine' cough

### 2. Bronchoscopy

- Rigid bronchoscopy + general anaesthesia for massive haemoptysis or removal of a foreign body

### 3. Endobrochial ultrasound

- To stage lung cancer

### 4. Thoracoscopy

- Gold standard for the evaluation of the pleural surfaces, characterisation of complex pleural effusion, and identification of exudate and haemorrhage
- For accurate staging of apical tumours

## Microbiological Investigations

- Sputum, pleural fluid, throat swabs, blood and bronchial washings and aspirates for **culture and sensitivity** for bacteria and fungi
- Molecular tests, such as nucleic acid amplification tests (NAATs) as first-line diagnostic tests for respiratory viruses, bacterial pathogens, such as _Legionella_ and _Myocplasma_

## Immunological and Serological Tests

- For diagnosis of **allergic asthma**, the measurement of IgE directed against specific antigens
- Serum precipitins for a reaction to **fungi** such as _Aspergillus_ or to antigens involved in **hypersensitivity pneumonitis**, such as **farmer's lung** or **bird fancier's lung**
- For diagnosis of **pneumonia**, pneumococcal antigen in siputum, blood or urine.
- For detection of **Respiratory viruses**, nose/throat swabs by immunofluorescence
- For diagnosis of _Legionella_ infection, detection of _Legionella_ antigen in urine.
- For marker of **fungal** infection, Beta-1,3-D-glucan detection (in blood)
- For diagnosis of **invasive aspergillosis**, _Aspergillus_ galactomannan (in blood and bronchial lavage fluid)
- For detection of **latent tuberculosis**, Infterferon-gamma release assyas (IGRA)

## Cytology and Histopathyology

- Cytological examination of exfoliated cells in pleural fluid or bronchia brushings and washings, or of fine needle aspirates from lymph nodes or pulmonary lesions, can support a diagnosis of malignancy
