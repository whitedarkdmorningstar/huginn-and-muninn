# Gout

> Gout is caused by deposition of monosodium urate monohydrate crystals in and around synovial joints.

- Gout is the most common inflammatory arthritis in men (\> 5:1 male preponderance)
- The risk of developing gout increases with age and with serum uric acid (SUA) levels
- Although hyperuricaemia is a strong risk factor for gout, only a minority of hyperuricaemic individuals actually develop gout

## Pathophysiology

![Uric acid metabolism](/v2/medicine/uric-acid-metabolism.png)

- One-third of the body uric acid pool is derived from dietary sources
- Two-thirds from endogenous purine metabolism
- In over 90% of patients, the main abnormality is reduced uric acid excretion by the kidney, which is genetically determined
- Impaired renal excretion of urate also accounts for the occurrence of hyperuricaemia in chronic renal failure and for hyperuricaemia associated with thiazide diuretic therapy

## Main Causes of Hyperuricaemia and Gout

### 1. Diminished renal function (reduce excretion)

1.  Renal failure
1.  Increased renal tubular reabsorption (usually genetically determined)
1.  Lead toxicity
1.  Lactic acidosis
1.  Alcohol
1.  Drugs
    - `Thiazide` and `loop diuretic`
    - `ACE inhibitor`
    - `Low-dose aspirin`
    - `Ciclosporin`
1.  `Pyrazinamide`
1.  `Cancer chemotherapy`

### 2. Increase intake

1.  Game
1.  Offal
1.  Shellfish
1.  Red meat

### 3. Increase production

1.  Myeloproliferative and lymphoproliferative disease
1.  Psoriasis
1.  High fructose intake
1.  Glycogen storage disease
1.  Inherited disorders: Lesch-Nyhan syndrome (pathogenic variants in HPRT)

## Clinical Features

### Classical presentation:

- Acute `monoarthritis` (first MTP joint in \> 50% cases)
  - May present as `polyarticular` in older women, and ultimately in men who have had repeated acute attacks of gout
- Other common sites: Ankle, midfoot, knee, small joints of hands, wrist and elbow
- Rare: Axial skeleton, larger proximal joints

### Typical presentation of acute gout:

- Onset: Rapid
- Timing: Worse in the early morning
- Severity: Reach maximum severity in 2-6 hours, ~~Severe pain~~ (often describe as the 'worst pain ever'), unable to wear a sock or to let a bedding rest on the joint
- Apperance: Marked synovitis, swelling and erythema
- Associated features: Fever, malaise and even delirium, especially if a large joint such as the knee is involved.
- Prognosis: Self-limiting over 5-14 days, with complete resolution. Pruritus and desquamation of overlying skin are common.

### Tophi

> Crystals may be deposited in the joints and soft tissues to produce irregular firm nodules called tophi.

- Extensor surfaces of fingers, hands, forearm, elbows, Achilles tendons and sometimes the helix of the ear.
- Tophi have a white colour, differentiating them from rheumatoid nodules.
- Tophi can ulcerate, discharging white gritty material, become infected or induce a local inflammatory response, with erythema and pus in the absence of secondary infection.
- _Patients with chronic renal failure_ may develop tophi within 12 months.
- _Patients on `thiazide` therapy who have coexisting `OA`_ may develop tophi without previous acute attacks

### Complications

- **Chronic hyperuricaemia** → Renal stone formation → Urate deposition in the kidney → Interstitial nephritis → ~~Renal impairment~~
- Common in _patients with chornic tohpaceous gout on diuretic therapy_

## Investigation

> Diagnosis of gout is confirmed by the identification of urate crystals in the aspirate from a joint, bursa or tophus.

### Synovial Fluid

- In acute gout, `turbid` due to ↑ neutrophil counts
- In chronic gout, `variable`, occassionally `white` due to urate crystals
- Even asymptomatic joint revels crystals.

### Other Tests

1. Renal function
1. Uric acid
   - May be normal during an attack because inflammation reduces serum urate level
1. ↑ESR, ↑CRP, Neutrophilia in acute attack and tophaceous gout
1. Glucose
1. Lipid profile
1. X-rays in _patients with chronic or tophaceous gout_

## Management

> 1. Deal with acute attack.
> 1. Lower serum uric acid and prevent further attack.

### Lifestyle Measures

- Lose weight
- Reduce alcohol intake, especially beer
- Avoid large amount of seafood, offal
- Several antihypertensive drugs including `thiazides`, `β-blockers` and `ACE inhibitors`, increase uric acid levels
- `losartan` has uricosuric effect

### Acute Gout

- Elevate the affected joint and apply ice
- **First choice**: PO `colchicine` (0.5 mg 2-4 times daily)
- PO `NSAIDs` are effective, but less commonly used because of CVS, CKD
- _Older adults with increased risk of toxicity with colchicine and NSAIDs_, PO `prednisolone` (15-20 mg daily for 2-3 days) or IM `methylprednisolone` (80-120mg daily)
- When first-line treatment are ineffective, IL-1 inhibition with `anakinra` or `canakinumab` (expensive)
- **Joint aspiration** can give pain relief, particularly if a large joint is affected and may be combined with an `intra-articular glucocorticoid` injection if the diagnosis is clear and infection has been excluded.

### Prophylaxis

> Patients who had more than one acute attack within 12 months and those with complications such as tophi or erosions.

> Target serum uric acid level: `<360 μmol/L (6mg/dL)` or `<300 μmol/L (5mg/dL)` in those with severe gout

- **First choice**: PO `Allopurinol`
- Starting dose: 100 mg daily, or half in _older patients_ and _renal impairment_
- Increase by 100 mg by 4 weeks until target uric acid level reached or side effect occurs or max dose reached (900 mg)

> Acute flare of gout often follows after initiation of urate-lowring therapy!

- Continue therapy even if an acute attack occurs.
- The risk of flares can be reduced by prophylaxis with oral `colchicine` (0.5-1mg daily) or an `NSAID` for the first few months.

#### Febuxostat

> Febuxostat undergoes hepatic metabolism, and no dose adjustment is required for renal impairment.

- More effective than `allopurinol`
- Commonly provoke acute attack when initiated
- Starting dose is 40-80mg daily, increasing to 120mg daily in _patients with an inadequate response_

<details>
<summary>Indications for urate-lowering drugs</summary>

- Recurrent attacks of acute gout (\> 1 attacks within 12 months)
- Evidence of bone or joint damage
- Tophi
- Renal impairment
- Nephrolithiasis
- Chemotherapy-related hyperuricemia

</details>

#### Uricosuric Drugs

- Uricosuric drugs, such as `probenecid`, `sulfinpyrazone` and `benzbromarone` are less commonly used.
- Require patient to take high fluid intake to avoid crystal deposition in renal tubule
- Contraindications: ~~Urate over-producers~~, ~~renal impairment~~ or ~~urolithiasis~~
