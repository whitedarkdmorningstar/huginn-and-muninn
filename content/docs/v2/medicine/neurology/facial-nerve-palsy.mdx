# Facial Nerve Palsy (Bell Palsy)

> Bell palsy is a lower motor neurone lesion of the 7th (facial) nerve, affecting all ages and both sexes.

- One of momst common causes of facial weakness
- Classically associated with _Herpes-virus infections_, _Lyme disease_
- Common following upper respiratory tract infections, during pregnancy and in _patients with diabetes_, immunosuppression and hypertension
- Can occur in HIV at the time of seroconversion.

## Causes

1. Infections
   - _Herpes-virus_**\*** (HSV-1)
   - _Lyme disease_**\***
   - Reactivation of _Varicella zoster virus_ (VZV) (Vesicles in the ear or on the palate) (Ramsay Hunt Syndrome)
   - Upper respiratory tract infections
   - HIV, TB, CMV, Syphillis
1. Traumatic & Iatrogenic
   - Skull bone / skull base fracture
   - Surgical procedure: Iatrogenic injury following parotid gland surgery, mastoidectomy, or acoustic neuroma removal
   - Dental procedure
1. Tumours
   - Parotid Gland Tumors / Malignancies
   - Vestibular Schwannoma / Acoustic Neuroma or other cerebellopontine angle masses
   - Lymphoma or meningeal carcinomatosis
1. Autoimmune & Inflammatory
   - **Guillain-Barré Syndrome** (and its variants like Miller Fisher syndrome)
   - **Sarcoidosis** (Neurosarcoidosis and Melkersson-Rosenthal syndrome)
   - **Sjögren's Disease**
1. Vascular & Metabolic
   - **Diabetes Mellitus**: Increases susceptibility to facial mononeuropathy.
   - **Brainstem Infarct**: Ischemia affecting the facial motor nucleus in the pons.

## Clinical Features

- The lesion is within the facial canal
- Onset: Subacute over a few hours
- Pain around th ear → unilateral facial weakness
- Patient may describe 'numb' in the face but there is no objective sensory loss (except the taste)
- Take impairment if _chorda tympani_ is involved
- Hyperacusis if _stapedius_ is involved

## Management

> `Glucocorticoids` within 72 hours of onset improve recovery

- `Prednisolone` 25 mg BD for 10 days, or 60 mg QID for 5 days followed by a taper to off in decrements of 10 mg per day

> Antiviral drugs are not effective!

- Artificial tears to prevent corneal drying
- Taping the eye shut overnight to prevent exposure keratitis and corneal abrasion
- Refer to an ophthalmologist if necessary

## Recovery

- 80% recover spontaneousy within 12 weeks
- Aberrant re-innervation may occur during recovery, producing unwanted facial movements:
  - Eye closure when the mouth is moved (synkinesis) or 'crocodile tears' (tearing during salivation)
- Recurrence is unusual
