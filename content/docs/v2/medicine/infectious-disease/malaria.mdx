# Malaria

> Malaria in humans is caused by _Plasmodium falciparum_, _P. vivax_, _P. ovale_, _P. malariae_ and _P. knowlesi_.

> Transmitted by the bite of female _Anopheles_ mosquitoes

## Pathogenesis

### Life cycle of the malarial parasite

- Mosquito bites on human blood containing **gametocytes** → Develop into **sporozoites** in 7-20 days → accumulates in salivary glands → bite and inoculate into the human blood stream.
- Within half an hour, **sporozoites** enter the liver → Develop into **merozoites** → After some days, **merozoites** invade RBCs → Asexual cycles of multiplication occurs → Produce **schizonts** → Rupture of **schizonts** releases more **merozoites** into the blood and causes **fever**, the periodicity.
- _P. vivax_ and _P. ovalue_ may persist in liver cells as dormant forms (**hypnozoites**), capable of developing into **merozoites** in months or years later.

![Life cycle of malarial parasite](/v2/medicine/malaria-parasite-life-cycle.png)

### Pathology

- RBCs invaded by malaria undergo **haemolysis** (most severe with _P. falciparum_)
- Anaemia is worsened by **dyserythropoiesis**, **splenomegaly** and depletion of folate stores.
- Vessel congestion results in organ damage, worsen by rupture of **schizonts**, releasing toxic and antigenic substances.

> _P. falciparum_ does not grow well in red cells that contain haemoglobin F, C or especially S.
> _P. vivax_ cannot enter red cells that lack the Duffy blood group; therefore many _West Africans_ and _African Americans_ are protected.

## Relationships between life cycle of parasite and clinical features of malaria

| Feature                                | _P. vivax_, _P. ovale_    | _P. malariae_         | _P. falciparum_       |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | --------------------- | --------------------- |
| Affected RBC age                       | Reticulocyte (young RBCs) | Senescent (old RBCs)  | All stages            |
| Pre-patent period (minimum incubation) | 8-25 days                 | 15-30 days            | 8-25 days             |
| Exo-erythrocytic cyle                  | Persistent as hypnozoites | Pre-erythrocytic only | Pre-erythrocytic only |
| Asexual cycle                          | 48 hrs synchronous        | 72 hrs synchronous    | < 48 hrs asynchronous |
| Fever periodicity                      | Alternate days            | Every third day       | None                  |
| Delayed onset                          | Common                    | Rare                  | Rare                  |
| Relapses                               | Common up to 2 years      | Many years later      | Up to 1 year          |

## Clinical Features

- Non-specific; Suspect anyone returning from an endemic area

### _P. falciparum_ infection

- ~~Most dangerous~~ of the malarias
- Insidious onset with malaise, headache, vomiting
- Cough and mild diarrhoea are common
- Fever has no particular pattern
- Jaundice, Hepatomegaly and splenomegaly with tenderness
- Anaemia and thrombocytopenia (rapidly)
- **Cerebral malaria**: Delirium, seizures, coma, usually without localizing signs
- Abortion and intrauterin growth retardation
- ↑ risk in previous splenectomy

### _P. vivax_ and _P. ovale_ infection

- Several days of continued fever with rigor
- Then, fever on alternate days
- **Classical bouts of fever**: Fever rises to about 40°C → 30-60 min later,, host or flush phase begins → After several hours, profuse perspiration and temperature falls gradually → repeated 48 hours later
- Tender hepatomegaly and splenomegaly
- Anaemia (slowly)
- Relapses frequent in the frist 2 years
- Maybe acquired from blood transfusion

### _P. malariae_ and _P. knowlesi_ infection

- Mild symptoms
- Fever every third day
- Occasional recrudescence of fever or without producing any symptoms
- Parasitemia persist many years → Glomerulonephritis and long-term nephrotic syndrome in children

## Investigations

- WHO recommends to confirm malaria diagnosis with **microscopy** or **Malaria rapid diagnostic test (RDT)** before initiating treatment.
- **Molecular methods (PCR)** are recommended in research or complex cases but not for routine clinical settings.

### 1. Microscopic Diagnosis

- **Giemsa-stained blood smears:**
  - **Thick smears:** Used to detect and quantify malaria parasites in the blood.
  - **Thin smears:** Used to identify the species of _Plasmodium_.
- **Benefits:**
  - Gold standard for malaria diagnosis.
  - Allows determination of parasitemia levels.

### 2. Rapid Diagnostic Tests (RDTs)

- Detect specific malaria antigens in blood (e.g., _Plasmodium falciparum_ histidine-rich protein 2 [PfHRP2], aldolase, or lactate dehydrogenase [LDH]).
- **Advantages:**
  - Quick results (15-30 minutes).
  - Useful in settings with limited laboratory facilities.
- **Limitations:**
  - May not differentiate species in non-falciparum malaria.
  - False negatives can occur with low parasitemia.

### 3. Molecular Diagnosis

- **Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR):**
  - Detects _Plasmodium_ DNA.
  - High sensitivity and specificity.
- **Use:**
  - Mainly in research
  - For determining whether a patient has a recrudescence of the same malaria parasite or a reinfection with a new parasite

### 4. Other Investigations (for Complications)

- **Full Blood Count (FBC):** To detect anemia and thrombocytopenia.
- **Liver Function Tests (LFTs):** To assess liver involvement in severe cases.
- **Renal Function Tests:** To monitor for acute kidney injury (AKI).
- **Blood Glucose:** To detect hypoglycemia in severe malaria.
- **Arterial Blood Gas (ABG):** For metabolic acidosis in severe malaria.
- **Imaging:** Chest X-ray may be required in pulmonary complications.

## Management

### Management of Complications

1. Coma
   - Maintain airway
   - Nurse on side
   - Exclude other treatable causes of coma (e.g. hypoglycaemia, bacterial meningitis)
   - Avoid harmful ancillary treatments such as `glucorticoids`, `heparin` and `adrenaline`
   - Intubate if necessary
1. Convulsions
   - Maintain airway
   - Treat promptly with `diazepam`
1. Shock ('algid malaria')
   - Suspect Gram-negative spesis
   - Take blood cultures
   - Give parenteral `antimicrobials`
   - Correct haemodynamic disturbance
1. Acute kidney injury
   - Exclude pre-renal causes
   - Fluid resuscitaton if appropriate
   - `Peritoneal dialysis` (`haemofiltration` or `haemodialysis` if available)
1. Acute pulmonary oedema
   - Nurse at 45°
   - Give oxygen, venesect[^1] 250 mL of blood
   - Give `diuretic`, Stop IV fluids
   - Intubate and PEEP/CPAP in life-threatening hypoxaemia
   - Haemofilter
1. Aspiration pneumonia
   - Give parenteral `antimicrobials`
   - Change position
   - Physiotherapy
   - Give oxygen
1. Hyperparasitaemia
   - Consider exchange transfusion (e.g. > 10% of circulating erythrocytes parasitised in non-immune patient with severe disease)
1. Metabolic acidosis
   - Exclude or treat hypoglycaemia, hypovolaemia and Gram-negative spesis
   - Fluid resuscitaton
   - Give oxygen
1. Hyperpyrexia
   - Tepid sponging, fanning, cooling blanket
   - Antipyretic drug (`paracetamol`)
1. Spontaneous bleeding and coagulopathy
   - Transfuse screened fresh whole blood (`cryoprecipitate`/`fresh frozen plasma` and `platelets` if available)
   - `Vit K` injection
1. Severe anaemia (packed cell volume < 15%)
   - Transfuse `fresh whole blood` or `packed cells` if pathogen screening of donor blood is available
1. Hypoglycemia
   - Measure blood glucose
   - Give `50% dextrose` injection followed by `10% dextrose` infusion (glucagon may be ineffective)

### Mild _P. falciparum_ malaria

- **Preferred therapy**:
  - `Co-artemether` (`Artemether` + `Lumefantrine`) 4 tabletsat 0, 8, 24, 36, 48 and 60 hrs
- **Alternative therapy**:
  - `Quinine` (600 mg of quinine salt TDS for 5-7 days), together with or followed by `Doxycycline` (200 mg OD for 7 days)
  - Use `Clindamycin` in pregnant woman or young child
- **Alternative therapy**:
  - `Atovaquone-Proguanil` (`Malarone` 4 tablets OD for 3 days)
- **Pregnancy**:
  - `Co-artemether` but ~~avoid in early pregnancy~~ **OR**
  - `Quinine` + `Clindamycin` (450 mg TDS for 7 days)

### Severe _falciparum_ malaria

> Early and appropriate antimalarial chemotherapy, active treatment of complications, correction of fluid, electrolyte and acid-base balance and avoidance of harmful ancillary treatments.

- **Preferred therapy**:
  - `Artesunate` 2.4 mg/kg IV at 0, 12 and 24 hrs, then OD for 7 days
  - Once patient can take orally, switch to 2mg/kg orally OD, to complete a total cumulative dose of 17-18 mg/kg
- **Alternative**:
  - `Quinine` loading dose 20 mg/kg IV over 4 hrs, up to a maximum of 1.4 g, then, maintenance dose of 10 mg/kg `quinine salt` given as 4-hr infusions 8 Hrly for the first 48 hrs, then 12 Hrly, up to a maximum of 700 mg per dose or until the patient can take orally
  - Combine with `Doxycycline` (Use `Clindamycin` in pregnancy or young child)
  - ~~Loading dose is contraindicated if `Quinine`, `Quinidine` or `Mefloquine` in previous 24 hrs~~
  - Monitor ECG

### Non-_falciparum_ malaria

- **Preferred therapy**:
  - `Chloroquine` (600 mg chloroquine base orally followed by 300 mg base[^2] in 6 hrs, then 150 mg base BD for 2 more days) + `Primaquine` ((30 mg orally daily for _P. vivax_) or (15 mg orallay daily for _P. ovale_) for 14 days after confirming G6PD-negative)
- **Alternative therapy**:
  - `Co-artemether` (as for _falciparum_) + `Primaquine` (same as above)
- **Patients with mild to moderate G6PD deficiency**:
  - `Chloroquine` + `Primaquine` 0.75 mg/kg weekly orally for 8 weeks
- **`Chloroquine`-resistant _P. vivax_**:
  - `Co-artemether` (as for _P. falciparum_)

## Prevention

- Choice of regimen is determined by area to be visited, length of stay, level of malaria transmission, level of drug resistance, presence of underlying disease in the traveller and concomitant medication taken

### 1. `Chloroquine` resistance high

- `Mefloquine` 250 mg weekly, 2-3 weeks before travel and continued 4 weeks after (~~Contraindicated in the first trimester of pregnancy, lactation, cardiac conduction disorders, epilepsy, psychiatric disorders; may cause neuropsychiatric disorders.~~) **OR**
- `Doxycycline` 100 mg daily, 1 week before and continued 4 weeks after (~~Causes photosensitisation and sunburn if high-protection sunblock is not used. Avoid in pregnancy.~~) **OR**
- `Atovaquone + Proguanil`(Malarone) 1 tablet daily, 1-2 days before travel until 1 week after return

### 2. `Chloroquine` resistance absent

- `Cloroquine` 300 mg base weekly + `Proguanil` 100-200 mg daily, 1 week before and continued until 4 weeks after travel
