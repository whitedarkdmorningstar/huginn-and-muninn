# Rabies

> Virus infects central nervous tissues and salivary glands of a wide range of mammals.

- Causal organism: `Rhabdovirus`
- Incubation period: **9 days to many months** (usually 4-8 weeks)
- Shorter incubation period if severe bites on the neck or head
- Mode of transmission: **Saliva** through bites, or licks on abrasions or intact mucous membranes
- Transmitted from dogs, bats, foxes

## Clinical Features

- Fever, parasthesia at the site of bite
- Prodromal features: 1-10 days
- Classically **hydrophobia**
- Anxious, delusion, hallucination, lucid interval
- Terminal hyperpyrexia (common)
- Death within a week of the onset of the symptoms

## Investigations

- Usually, **clinically**
- Rapid immunofluorescent techniques to detect antigen in corneal impression smear or skin biopsies

## Management

### Established Disease

- Post-exposure prophylaxis
- ICU care for respiratory and cardic failure
- Heavily sedated with `diazepam` (+ `chlorpromazine`)
- Entral or parentral nutritions

### Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP)

- For those who handle potentially infected animals

### Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP)

> Neither vaccine nor RIG is needed for exposure within 3 months after completed rabies prophylaxis!

- PEP should be started immediately after the bite.
- If the animal (dogs, cats only) remains healthy throughout observation of 10 days, PEP may be modified to PrEP vaccination.
- Consumption of meat or milk from a rabid animal is strongly discouraged and should be avoided but if it occurs, PEP is not indicated.

#### Assessment

- Wound severity
- Risk of transmission
- Previous immunization exposure/Pre-exposure prophylaxis (PEP)

#### Categorization of Exposue

##### Category 1 (No Risk)

- Animal licks on intack skin
- Touching or feeding animals

> Reassurance only, No vaccine needed

##### Category 2 (Moderate Risk)

- Nibbling of uncovered skin
- Minor scratches or abrasions without bleeding

> Wound management + Immediate vaccination

> Aminister Rabies Immunoglobulin in case of immuno-compromised patients (patient on steroids, chloroquine and chemotherapy for malignant diseases, HIV/AIDS patients)

##### Category 3 (Severe Exposure)

- Single or multiple wounds on head and neck
- Single or multiple transdermal bites or scratches, laceration with bleeding
- Licks on borken skin
- Contamination of mucous membrane or broken skin with saliva from animal
- Exposures due to direct contact with bats

> Wound management + Immediate vaccination + Rabies Imuunoglobulin

#### Wound Management

- Clean the wound with soap, or detergent and water, or antiseptic (e.g., `povidine iodine`)
- Wound irrigation and debridement if necessary
- Wound should not be sutured as suturing may push the virus deeper.
- In case of severe facial bite. (e.g., a torn pinna, nose or eyelid), RIG should be infiltrated, and delayed for several hours to allow diffusion of the RIG, and loose sutures may be applied. Proper suturing may be done after 2-3 days.
- Antibiotics to prevent sepsis:
  - PO `Co-amoxiclav 625 mg` TDS for 7 days **OR** IV or PO `Flucloxacillin + Amoxicillin 500 mg` 8 Hrly for 7 days
  - For penicillin allergy, IV `Ceftriaxone 1 G` 12 Hrly **OR** PO `cefixime 200 mg` BD for 7 days

#### Rabies Immunoglobulin (RIG)

##### Indications

- For all category 3, and category 2 in immunocompromised patients.

> RIG is not indicated for previous completed PrEP, and at least 2 administration of vaccine for PEP!

> Cases with highest priority range for RIG administrations:
>
> 1. Category III exposed patients with multiple bites
> 2. Those with deep wounds, or bites to highly innervated parts of the body, such as the head, neck and hands
> 3. Patients with severe immunodeficiency
> 4. Cases where the biting animal is a confirmed or probable rabies case, or where bites, scratches or exposure of a mucous membrane are caused by a bat

##### Timing

- RIG should be administered before starting anti-rabies vaccination or at the same time.
- RIG can be administered up to 7 day of first dose of vaccination. After 7 days, antibodies are present and RIG is not recommended.

##### Dosage

- `Equine rabies immunoglobulin (ERIG) 40 IU/kg` (Max 1200 IU)
- `Human rabies immunoglobulin (HRIG) 20 IU/kg` (Max 4000 IU)

##### Administration

- Administer into and around the wound. _Avoid multiple injection into the wound._
- For small wounds, the maximal quantity that is anatomically feasible should be administered. (_if large volumes of RIG are injected into a small body area with limited tissue, `compartment syndrome` can occur._)
- For large and multiple wounds, RIG can be diluted with physiological buffered saline
- For mucosal exposures with no wound, further rinsing with diluted RIG can be considered.
- _WHO no longer recommends injecting the remainder of the calculated RIG dose IM at a distance from the wound._
- ~~RIG should never be administered in the same syringe or at the same anatomical site as vaccine.~~

##### For Anaphylactic Reactions

- SC/IM 0.5 ml of adrenaline 1:1000 for adults
- SC/IM 0.01 ml/kg of adrenaline 1:1000 for children

#### Rabies Vaccine

> If _already vaccinated_, only booster dose on `days 0 and 3`. If previous IM, prefer IM route. If previously ID, prefer ID route.

> Pregnancy, lactation, infancy, old age and concurrent illness are not contraindications.

##### Intrauscular Regimen: **Zagreb** Regimen (2-1-1)

> IM one vial on each deltoid muscles on day 0 and one vial on each of days 7 and 21

- Day 0 is the first dose administration of rabies vaccine; not the day of the exposure/dog bite.

##### Intrauscular Regimen: **Essen** Regimen

> `1 ml` in the deltoid area (anterolateral thigh for children) on `day 0, 3,7, 14` (+ `day 28` in immune suppressed patient).

##### Intradermal Regimen: **Thai Red Cross** Regimen (2-2-2-0-2)

> ID `0.1 ml` of reconstituted vaccine on `each deltoid area` on `days 0, 3, 7 and 28`.

> Immunocompromised patients should be given by IM route!

- Day 0 is the first dose administration of rabies vaccine; not the day of the exposure/dog bite.

- Reconstituted vaccines must be used as soon as possible, or within 6 hours if stored 2-8°C
- A palpable bleb in the skin should be seen after given ID, if not, repeat the injection slightly away from the first one.
