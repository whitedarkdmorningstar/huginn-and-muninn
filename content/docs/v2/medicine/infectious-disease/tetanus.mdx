# Tetanus

## Aetiology

### Causal Organism

- _Clostridium tetani_
- **Gram-positive, spore-forming, obligate anaerobic bacillus**
- A commensal in the gut of humans and domestic animals that is found in soil

### Incubation Period

- **3-21 days** (average **7-10 days**).
- ~~Shorter incubation = More severe attack = worse prognosis~~.

### Mode of Entery

- **Spores enter the body** through wounds

## Pathogenesis

- **Spores enter the body** through wounds
- Spores germinate and bacilli multiply only in the anaerobic conditions:
  - Areas of tissue necrosis
  - If the oxygen tension is lowered by the presence of other organisms, particularly if aerobic
- The bacilli remain localised but produce an exotoxin with an affnity for motor nerve endings and motor nerve cells
- Exotoxin has passed into the blood stream → Rigidity and tonic muscle spasms

## Clinical Features

- Most early symptom is **trismus** (Painless lockjaw), inability to open mouth ← masster muscle spasm
- Tonic rigity spreads and involves the muscle of the face, neck and back → **Risus sardonicus** (sardonic smile), and **Opisthotonus** (severe hyperextension and arching of the back) and **board-like abdominal rigidity**
- In severe cases, **violent spasms** lasting for a few seconds to 3-4 minutes occur (spontaneously, or by stimuli such as movement or noise) → Exhaustion, painful → After 1 week, ↑ in frequency and severity → **Death** from exhaustion, asphyxia or aspiration pneumonia.
- In less severe cases, the spasms does not occur after a week or not at all
- In rare cases, **localised tetanus**: stiffness or spasms of the muscles nearby the wound
- Autonomic involvment → CVS complications (hypertension)

<summary>Other lockjaw causes</summary>

- **Painful lockjaw**: Dental abscess, Septic throat
- Functional neurological disorders, Phenothiazine overdose, Injection drug user overdose

</details>

## Investigations

> Diagnosis is made clinically.

- **PCR and Wound culture for _C. tetani_** from wound swab or tissue sample

## Management

### Established Disease

1. Neutralize absorbed toxin
   - Human tetanus immunoglobulin (`HTIG`) 500 IU IM or IV **OR**
   - `IVIG` 3,000-6,000 IU IM or IV
1. Prevent further toxin production
   - Wound debridement
   - Antibiotics
     - `Metronidazole` 500 mg IV 6-8 Hrly for 7-10 days **OR**
     - `Penicillin G` 1-2 million units IV 4-6 Hrly for 7-10 days
     - Alternative antibiotics include `tetracyclines`, `macrolides`, `clindamycin`, `cephalosporins` and `chloramphenicol`
1. Control muscle spasms
   - Nurse in a quiet room
   - Avoid unnecessary stimuli
   - Give IV `diazepam`
   - If spasms continue, paralyse patient and ventilate
1. General measures
   - Maintain hydration and nutrition
   - Treat secondary infections
   - Vaccination following recovery

### Prevention

#### Active Immunization (Tetanus Toxoid - TT)

- **Primary series**: 3 doses (at 6, 10, 14 weeks).
- **Booster doses**: At **5 years, 10 years, then every 10 years**.

#### Post-Exposure Prophylaxis

| Wound Type         | Vaccination Status       | Management                                             |
| ------------------ | ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Clean, minor wound | ≥ 3 TT doses             | No TIG, TT booster if \> 10 years since last dose      |
| Clean, minor wound | \< 3 TT doses or unknown | TT booster, no TIG                                     |
| Contaminated wound | ≥ 3 TT doses             | TT booster if \> 5 years since last dose, consider TIG |
| Contaminated wound | \< 3 TT doses or unknown | TT booster + TIG                                       |
