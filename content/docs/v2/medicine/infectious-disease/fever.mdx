# Fever

- Elevated core body temperature > 38.0°C (100.4°F)
- A response to cytokines and acute phase proteins
- Occurs both in infections and non-infectious conditions (e.g., autoimmune diseases, malignancy, drug reactions)

## Clinical Assessment

1. Documentation of fever
   - Diagnose only when body temperature > 38.0°C (100.4°F)
   - Axillary and aural measurement is less accurate than oral or rectal

1. Rigors
   - Shivering (followed by excessive sweating) occurs with a rapid rise in body temperature from any cause

1. Night Sweats
   - Sweating from any cause is worse at night
   - Associated with particular infections (e.g., tuberculosis, endocarditis)

1. Excessive Sweating
   - Excessive environmental heat, alcohol, anxiety
   - Diabetes, thyrotoxicosis, acromegaly, lymphoma

1. Recurrent Fever
   - e.g. Borrelia recurrentis, bacterial abscesses, malaria, autoimmune diseases, malignancy

1. Accompanying Features
   - Severe headache and photophobia: meningitis, encephalitis including parameningeal infections such as sinusitis, mastoiditis
   - Delirium: common in young children and older people
   - Myalgia: viral infections such as influenza, sepsis including meningococcal sepsis
   - Shock: severe infections and sepsis

## History-taking in Suspected Infectious Disease

1. Presenting Complaint
   - Duration

1. Review of Systems
   - Must be comprehensive

1. Past Medical History
   - Define the 'host' and comorbidities
   - Surgical and dental procedures involving prosthetic material (e.g., heart valves, joint replacements, vascular grafts)
   - Previous infections and antimicrobial resistant infections

1. Medication History
   - Non-prescription drugs, use of antimicrobials and immunosuppressants
   - Medications that interact with antimicrobials
   - Medications that can cause fever (e.g., antipsychotics, antibiotics, anticonvulsants, allopurinol, heparin)

1. Allergy History
   - Especially to antimicrobials, allergic manifestations

1. Family and Contact History
   - Infections and their duration
   - Sensitively explore exposure to key infections, e.g. TB and HIV

1. Travel History
   - Countries visited
   - Previously resident

1. Occupation
   - e.g. Antrax in leather tannery workers

1. Recreational Pursuits
   - e.g. Leptospirosis in canoeists and windsurfers

1. Animal Exposures
   - Pets

1. Dietary History
   - Under-cooked meats, shellfish, unpasteurized diary products, well water
   - Establish who else was exposed

1. History of IV Drug Injection or Receipt of Blood Products
   - Risks for blood-borne viruses

1. Sexual History

1. Vaccination History and Use of Prophylactic Medicines
   - Ocuupations or age-related vaccines
