# Nasogastric Tubes

> These tubes are passed into the stomach via the nose.
> For drainage, large (16F) but uncomfortable
> For feeding, small (10F)

## Indications

- To decompress the stomach/GI tract (especially in obstruction, e.g., GOO, ileus, intestinal obstruction)
- For gastric lavage
- To administer feed/drugs (especially in critically ill patients or those with dysphagia, e.g., motor neuron disease, post CVA)

## Contraindications

- Any suspicion of a facial fracture

## Cautions

- Recent upper GI surgery

## Procedure

1. Get patient's consent
1. Wash your hand
1. Prepare the instruments:
   1. Non-sterile gloves and an apron
   1. A new, cool tube
   1. A cup of water to hand
   1. Lubricant gel
   1. pH paper to confirm
   1. Tape to secure the nose
1. Wear **non-sterile** gloves and an apron (to protect both you and the patient)
1. Explain the procedure
1. Measure the tube length to be inserted
   - Place the tip of the NGT at the **tip of the patient's nose**
   - Extend the tube along the side of the head to the **earlobe**
   - Continue down to the **Xiphoid process** (or typically 5 cm below the Xiphoid process for proper placement)
   - Note the markings (the stomach is \~35-40 cm in adults; so markings should be greather than that length)
1. Place the lubricated tube in nostril with its natural curve promoting passage down, rather than up. (**Right nostril is often easier than the left**)
1. When the tip is estimated to be entering the throat, rotate the tube by \~180° to discourage passage into the mouth
1. Ask the patient to swallow a sip of water, and advance as they do, timing each push with a swallow.
1. If this fails, try the other nostril.
1. Tape securely to the nose.

## Confirming Position

> Misplaced NGT have led to a number to preventable deaths!!

- Aspirate \> 0.5 ml gastric contents via tube
  - Withdrawing or advancing a few cm or turning the patient on the left side to help
- Use pH paper to test (Turn hot colors in acidic and dark colors in alkali)
- Gastric contents are acid (pH ≤ 5.5) although antacids or PPIs may increase the pH
- If pH \> 5.5 and NGT is needed for drugs or feed, then position must be checked radiologically.

> The 'whoosh' test is NOT an accepted method of testing for tube position!

## Complications

- Pain
- Loss of electrolytes
- Oesophagitis
- Tracheal or duodenal intubation
- Necrosis: retro- or nasopharyngeal
- Stomach perforation

## Weaning

- In removal of an NGT in situ for decompressiion or relief of obstruction, wean it so that the patient manages well without it.
- Drainage should be \< 750 ml/24 hours output for successful weaning.
- First, it should be on free drainage with, e.g. 4 hrly aspirations
- Then, spigot with 4 hrly aspirations
- Then, spigot only. If it is tolerated along with oral intake, then it is safe to remove. If not, take a step backwards.
